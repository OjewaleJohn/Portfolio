# Quantitative Content Analysis of Human Trafficking Coverage

## Project Overview

This undergraduate research project examined how **The Punch** and **The Guardian** newspapers reported human trafficking. It used a structured quantitative content-analysis approach to measure **coverage frequency, story prominence, journalistic genre, direction of reportage, and forms of human trafficking reported**.

The project demonstrates an early foundation in data collection, coding, classification, descriptive analysis, cross-tabulation, interpretation, and evidence-based reporting.

## Research Objectives

The study sought to:

1. Determine the frequency of human-trafficking reportage in the selected newspapers.
2. Assess the prominence given to human-trafficking stories.
3. Examine the journalistic genres used in reporting the stories.
4. Identify the forms of human trafficking most frequently reported.

## Data Scope

- **Publications:** The Punch and The Guardian
- **Study period:** 2019-2020
- **Population:** 1,462 newspaper issues
- **Sample:** 400 newspaper issues
- **Allocation:** 200 issues from each newspaper
- **Relevant human-trafficking items identified:** 282

The study used simple random sampling and a structured coding sheet to classify relevant newspaper content.

## Variables Analysed

- Newspaper
- Frequency of coverage
- Story prominence / page placement
- Journalistic genre
- Direction of reportage
- Form of human trafficking reported

### Prominence Categories

### Journalistic Genres

### Direction Categories
Positive | Negative | Neutral

### Forms of Human Trafficking
Forced labour | Forced criminal activities | Sexual exploitation of women | Removal of organs | People smuggling

## Analytical Approach

The project followed a clear analytical workflow:

**Research question -> sampling -> data collection -> coding -> categorisation -> frequency/percentage analysis -> cross-tabulation -> interpretation -> recommendations**

The submitted thesis explicitly reports the use of frequencies, percentages, and cross-tabulations. I also used SPSS during the project; however, the submitted thesis does not explicitly name the software.

## Key Findings

### Coverage Frequency
- **The Punch:** 177 stories (62.8%)
- **The Guardian:** 105 stories (37.2%)

### Story Prominence
- **Inside pages:** 86.5%
- **Front page:** 12.1%
- **Back page:** 1.4%

### Journalistic Genre
- **Straight news:** 68.1%
- **Picture news:** 16.3%
- **Feature:** 10.3%
- **Editorial cartoon:** 3.2%
- **Editorial:** 2.1%

### Forms of Human Trafficking Reported
- **Forced criminal activities:** 50.7%
- **Sexual exploitation of women:** 25.2%
- **Forced labour:** 12.4%
- **People smuggling:** 10.3%
- **Removal of organs:** 1.4%

### Direction of Coverage
- **Positive:** 63.8%
- **Negative:** 28.7%
- **Neutral:** 7.4%

## Interpretation

The Punch accounted for a larger share of the identified human-trafficking stories. However, most stories appeared on inside pages, suggesting relatively low prominence. Straight news dominated the coverage, while features and editorials were used much less frequently, indicating limited in-depth treatment of the subject.
